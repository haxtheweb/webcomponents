Couldn't sleep, so I walked. The fog had come up off the river and swallowed the whole block except for one streetlight, which stood in it like a man holding a lantern who has forgotten what he came out to find.

I stood under it long enough to finish a cigarette I no longer smoke. Someone across the street stood with me the whole time and never once came into the light. Nobody else was out. The city at that hour is a set waiting for actors.

On the way home I worked out the ending I have been circling for a month. It is not a twist. It is the opposite of a twist: the thing everyone expected, done so plainly that it hurts. I wrote it down the moment I got in, before the coat came off.

It is the first page in weeks that I have read back and not wanted to burn change.
